In short

What we are building

Helixona is an integrative medicine clinic in Irvine, California. Its flagship offer is the Complex Chronic Program: nine months of physician-led care for people with persistent fatigue, autoimmune conditions, Lyme, mold illness, long COVID and similar complex problems. A patient pays a separate assessment fee (Month 0), then $2,000 a month for nine months, and may continue afterwards if the physician and patient agree.

The clinic’s medical record, eClinicalWorks (eCW), stays the legal chart and the calendar for insurance visits. Everything else the program needs is being built as a patient platform, used by patients on their phone and by staff on desktop:

IntakeWebsite inquiry, screening, the physician’s questionnaire, document uploads, ten consents with e-signature, chart prep into eCW.
Daily careDaily health check-in, crash or flare reports, alerts to the nurses with response targets, patient history over time.
BookingsProgram sessions on rooms and devices by Plan of Care rules; single therapies anyone can book and pay from the website; a copy of each booking in eCW.
Membership and moneyAgreement, card or bank on file, monthly charge, failed-payment handling, Month 9 review, receipts. Stripe for payments.
CommunicationsEvery email and text a patient receives, from invitation to receipts, with approved wording.
eCW botA bot that places the intake note and PDF in the chart, mirrors bookings into the calendar and reads it back every morning, checking its own work.

The clickable mockups of every screen already exist. What we are missing is the detail of how each flow works today and how it should work in the platform: who does what, in what order, with which rule, and what the patient sees. That is what we need from you.

The ask

The flows

Thirteen flows and who to sit with

F0

The full eCW schedule: every provider, room and therapy

Sit with: Mira (front desk) · Charlene · the eCW administrator

Why first: the platform will book program sessions and single therapies on its own calendar, and eCW will keep taking bookings for insurance visits and for whatever the front desk still books by hand. To never offer a slot that eCW has already given away, the platform must read the complete eCW schedule, all staff and all therapies, before each booking and every morning. Every booking flow (F7, F8) and the bot’s mirror (F12) depends on this.

We need
  • Every calendar that exists in eCW today: each physician, NP, nurse, therapist, room, device and therapy. Which of them are set up as providers, which as resources, and which are not in eCW at all (booked on paper, a whiteboard or a spreadsheet)
  • Screen share: how the front desk views the whole day and the whole week for all staff at once, and whether that view can be printed or exported (reports, Excel, PDF) with every column
  • What each appointment carries: start and end time, provider or resource, visit type, reason, status (scheduled, confirmed, arrived, checked out, cancelled, no-show), who booked it and when it last changed
  • Everything that occupies time without being an appointment: blocked time, lunch, holidays, provider availability templates, double-booking and overbooking rules, waiting lists
  • How often the schedule changes during the day, who changes it (front desk, providers, call center, patients through healow) and how the team finds out today
  • The collision rule we should apply: how far ahead to read (for example 60 days), how often (every morning plus a fresh read right before each booking), what counts as a conflict for a room, a device or a person, and who resolves one when it happens
  • Whether eCW offers any export or interface for the schedule (scheduled reports, HL7 SIU messages, FHIR R4, healow Open Access) and what it costs, since that would replace screen reading by the bot

Fixed points

Decisions already made

  • Pilot with 3 to 5 patients first.
  • Clinical content (questionnaire, check-ins, alert rules) is Dr. D’s and is implemented exactly as she supplies it.
  • Patients never self-enroll in the program; Complex Chronic always goes through a screening call.
  • The platform owns rooms, devices and program bookings; eCW receives a copy. Insurance visits stay in eCW.
  • The platform never offers a slot without first reading the full eCW schedule; a conflict goes to a person, never to the patient.
  • Stripe for all payments. Month 0 is a separate charge from the $2,000 × 9 membership.
  • A failed payment never discharges a patient or changes care by itself.
  • The platform is reviewed during office hours and never presents itself as monitoring; every check-in says so.
